## Match body style to work

Vehicle type should follow passenger, load, route and access requirements. Comparing body styles without a defined duty cycle risks paying for capability that is not used—or selecting a vehicle that cannot perform the task safely.

## Saloon

A saloon may suit frequent intercity travel, client-facing duties and users who value stable road manners and a separate luggage area. Check rear-seat access, luggage opening and urban parking against the actual role.

## SUV

An SUV can provide an elevated seating position, flexible cabin and easier entry for some users. It should not be selected on appearance alone: compare energy use, tyre cost, dimensions and whether additional ride height is operationally necessary.

## Light commercial vehicle

Panel vans and combi vehicles serve tools, goods, technical teams or mixed passenger-and-load work. Define payload, usable dimensions, door configuration, load restraint and site restrictions. Tax, speed, licence and access rules may differ and require current professional verification.

## Apply a common scorecard

Score safety, task fit, driver ergonomics, energy, serviceability, downtime and total cost using weighted requirements. Conduct a controlled trial with realistic loads and routes where the decision is material.

## Standardise intelligently

A limited choice list can simplify procurement and support, but retain separate vehicle classes where duties genuinely differ. Review utilisation and operating feedback so the policy evolves with the organisation.

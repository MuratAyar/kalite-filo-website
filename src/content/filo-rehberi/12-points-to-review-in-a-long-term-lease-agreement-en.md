## 1. Vehicle and specification

Record the exact model, powertrain, trim, equipment and any permitted substitution. A model-family description alone may not define the vehicle the operation expects to receive.

## 2. Contract term and delivery

Confirm when the term starts, the planned delivery date and what applies if delivery is delayed. Distinguish an estimated date from a binding commitment.

## 3. Mileage allowance

Check total or annual mileage, whether vehicles can pool distance, and the rates for excess and under-mileage. Understand when the allowance can be amended.

## 4. Maintenance scope

Identify scheduled servicing, wear items, breakdowns and exclusions. Note who selects the workshop, approves work and manages appointments.

## 5. Tyres

Specify quantity, type, seasonal requirements, replacement criteria, storage and damage exclusions. “Tyres included” is not sufficiently precise on its own.

## 6. Insurance and claims

Review cover, deductibles, driver restrictions, notification deadlines and uninsured events. Establish how claim history or repeated damage affects cost.

## 7. Replacement mobility

Confirm eligibility, waiting period, vehicle class, maximum duration and geographic limits. A replacement vehicle may not be automatic for every workshop visit.

## 8. Roadside assistance

Check operating hours, coverage area, towing destination and support for punctures, flat batteries or driver error.

## 9. Taxes, fines and administration

Clarify which taxes and statutory charges are included and what administration fees apply to fines, tolls or document processing.

## 10. Early termination and change

Model the cost of reducing fleet size, changing term or mileage and returning a vehicle early. Organisational change does not automatically remove contractual liability.

## 11. Return condition

Obtain the fair-wear standard and inspection process before signing. Define required documents, keys, equipment and the method for challenging an assessment.

## 12. Data, service levels and escalation

Set reporting frequency, data access, response targets and named escalation routes. The agreement should make day-to-day governance as clear as the commercial price.

Have legal, finance, tax and operational specialists review the final agreement for the company's circumstances. This guide is a practical checklist, not legal advice.

## Use evidence instead of estimates

The mileage allowance in a lease affects both the monthly rental and the financial position at contract end. Set it from odometer history, route plans and role-specific travel rather than a broad company average. Include seasonal peaks, planned expansion and regular journeys that may not appear in expense records.

## Calculate at vehicle level

Group comparable users, then test each vehicle against the group assumption. New roles need a documented forecast using working days, average daily distance and known long journeys. Add a justified contingency, not an arbitrary percentage that conceals weak data.

## Understand contract mechanics

Confirm whether mileage is measured per vehicle or pooled, how excess and under-mileage are priced, whether bands can be revised and when changes must be requested. A low allowance may reduce the headline rental but create a larger end-of-term charge. An unnecessarily high allowance can mean paying for capacity that is never used.

## Monitor throughout the term

Capture odometer readings consistently and compare actual mileage with a time-phased target. Investigate variance early. Work can sometimes be redistributed between suitable vehicles, while a material and lasting change may justify discussing a contract amendment with the provider.

## Keep an audit trail

Record the forecast, assumptions, approvals and subsequent changes. This gives finance teams a defensible accrual and helps fleet managers improve future tenders. Mileage management is not an end-of-contract exercise; it is a routine control throughout the lease.

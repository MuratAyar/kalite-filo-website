## Tyres are an operating system

Tyres influence braking, steering, energy consumption, comfort and availability. Manage them through policy, inspection and data rather than only replacing them after a defect.

## Define the standard

Specify approved sizes and ratings, seasonal policy, inspection frequency and replacement criteria based on current law, manufacturer requirements and operating conditions. Legal minimums are not automatically the right operational threshold.

## Make drivers part of the control

Drivers should know how to identify pressure warnings, visible damage and abnormal vibration, and where to report them. Pressure checks must follow the vehicle guidance and be performed safely.

## Plan by mileage and season

Forecast replacement demand using actual wear and annual distance. Book seasonal changes early, record stored sets and coordinate tyre work with servicing when this does not compromise safety or timing.

## Measure cost correctly

Track cost per kilometre, premature replacement, punctures, downtime and roadside events by vehicle and tyre type. A lower purchase price may not mean a lower whole-life cost if durability or availability is poorer.

## Investigate unusual wear

Repeated shoulder wear, uneven tread or frequent damage may point to pressure, alignment, load, route or driving issues. Correct the cause as well as replacing the tyre. Keep auditable records of inspections and work performed.

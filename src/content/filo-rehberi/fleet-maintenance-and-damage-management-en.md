## Treat availability as an operating measure

Maintenance and damage management should keep safe vehicles available for work, not merely process invoices. Track planned and unplanned downtime, repeat repairs, overdue services and the time between incident notification and return to service.

## Plan preventive work

Use manufacturer requirements, mileage and operating conditions to forecast service dates. Schedule work before critical travel periods and notify drivers early. Tyres, inspections and recurring defects belong in the same plan so that workshop visits can be coordinated where appropriate.

## Standardise damage reporting

Give drivers a clear first-response procedure covering safety, emergency support, photographs, third-party details and prompt notification. The organisation should then triage drivability, arrange recovery or repair, record approvals and maintain communication with the driver and relevant providers.

## Control repair quality and time

Agree service levels, escalation routes and evidence requirements with workshops or leasing partners. Monitor waiting time for assessment, approval, parts and repair separately; this shows where vehicles are actually being delayed. Validate completion and update the vehicle record before return to operation.

## Learn from recurring events

Analyse incidents by vehicle, location, cause, driver group and cost. The purpose is prevention: training, route design, parking arrangements or vehicle specification may need to change. A documented, non-punitive reporting culture improves data quality and supports safer, more reliable fleet operations.

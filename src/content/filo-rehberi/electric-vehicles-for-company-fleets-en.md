## Start with the duty cycle

An electric vehicle is most convincing when its daily work can be completed reliably with practical charging. Map distance, stops, parking duration, payload and exceptional journeys for each role. Average mileage alone can hide a small number of days that determine operational feasibility.

## Assess charging before ordering vehicles

Identify home, workplace and public charging options, electrical capacity, access control and payment arrangements. The required charger count depends on energy demand and dwell time, not simply the number of vehicles. Plan for maintenance, failed sessions and future growth.

## Model real operating cost

Compare lease or capital cost, electricity tariffs, charging equipment, network fees, maintenance, tyres and insurance. Consumption varies with speed, temperature, load and auxiliary use. Include the cost and employee time involved in public charging where it is part of the operating model.

## Manage people and process

Drivers need clear guidance on charging, route planning, cable security and incident reporting. Define who pays for home electricity, how business use is evidenced and what happens when a charger is unavailable. A controlled pilot can test these processes before wider deployment.

## Use a mixed transition where appropriate

Electrification need not be all-or-nothing. Assign electric vehicles to compatible routes first and retain other powertrains where charging or distance constraints remain material. Review telematics and charging data after launch, then expand on the basis of measured reliability, cost and user experience.

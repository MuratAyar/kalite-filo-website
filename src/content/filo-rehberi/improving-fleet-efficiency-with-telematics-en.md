## Begin with a business question

Telematics can record location, mileage, speed, idling, energy use and vehicle events. Do not collect data simply because it is available. Define the operational or safety decision each measure is intended to support.

## Establish a reliable baseline

Check installation, vehicle assignment and data quality before comparing drivers or models. Segment results by route, payload and duty cycle; context is essential when interpreting consumption or driving events.

## Target useful interventions

Idling reports may support route or working-practice changes. Mileage and diagnostic data can improve service scheduling. Repeated harsh events may indicate a need for coaching, road-risk review or a different vehicle specification. Use trends and corroborating evidence rather than isolated alerts.

## Engage drivers fairly

Explain what is collected, why, who can access it and how long it is retained. Set transparent thresholds and give drivers an opportunity to understand results. Recognition and coaching generally support lasting improvement better than unexplained scoring.

## Protect data

Apply access controls, retention limits and supplier due diligence. Location and driver information may be personal data; obtain qualified advice and meet applicable information and legal obligations before deployment.

## Measure outcomes

Track whether interventions reduce consumption, incidents, downtime or unnecessary mileage. Remove reports that do not lead to action. A successful telematics programme is measured by better decisions and operational outcomes, not by the volume of data collected.

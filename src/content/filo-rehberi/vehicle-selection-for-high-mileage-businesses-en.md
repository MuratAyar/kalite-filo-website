## Small differences scale quickly

At 40,000–50,000 kilometres a year or more, modest changes in consumption, service frequency, tyre life and driver fatigue can have a significant cumulative effect. Evaluate the full duty cycle, not a short demonstration drive.

## Prioritise seating and controls

Seat support, adjustment range, visibility, noise, climate performance and driver-assistance systems matter during long working days. Confirm that drivers of different sizes can achieve a safe position and use controls without distraction.

## Model route-specific efficiency

Motorway, urban and mixed routes produce different results for petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric powertrains. Use actual speed, stop frequency, payload and charging opportunity. Test energy-price and winter-consumption scenarios where relevant.

## Plan service continuity

High mileage brings service intervals and tyre replacement forward. Assess workshop coverage, appointment lead time, parts availability, roadside assistance and replacement-mobility terms. A technically efficient vehicle can still be costly if it spends too long out of service.

## Set an appropriate contract

Use evidence to align mileage allowance, contract term and fair-wear expectations. Review odometers monthly and act early on sustained variance. For owned vehicles, consider how accumulated mileage affects maintenance risk and disposal timing.

## Measure after deployment

Track consumption, downtime, incidents, driver feedback and cost per kilometre by model and route. Use the evidence to refine the approved vehicle list rather than treating selection as a one-time procurement decision.

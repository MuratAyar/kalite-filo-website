## 1. Vehicle downtime

Lost working time, replacement travel and delayed service can exceed the workshop invoice. Measure hours or days unavailable as well as repair cost.

## 2. Excess mileage

Contract mileage that is set too low can create a material end-of-term liability. Monitor odometers against a time-phased allowance.

## 3. Under-utilisation

A vehicle can be fully paid for while creating little operational value. Review utilisation and whether work can be consolidated safely.

## 4. Driver behaviour

Harsh acceleration, idling, speeding and poor tyre care can increase energy, wear and incident exposure. Use proportionate data, coaching and clear policy.

## 5. Administration time

Booking services, checking invoices, processing fines and coordinating incidents consume employee time. Estimate this workload even if it does not have a dedicated ledger code.

## 6. Return-condition charges

Missing equipment, unreported damage and poor preparation can increase lease-return cost. Inspect vehicles early and use the contractual fair-wear standard.

## 7. Unplanned replacement mobility

When contracted replacement cover is unavailable or unsuitable, taxis, short rentals and employee expenses can accumulate across the fleet.

## 8. Poor specification

An unsuitable vehicle may use more energy, carry loads inefficiently or create repeated modifications and downtime. Correct requirement definition is a cost control.

Assign each hidden cost an owner, data source and review frequency. Visibility is the first step; the goal is to change decisions and processes that create avoidable cost.

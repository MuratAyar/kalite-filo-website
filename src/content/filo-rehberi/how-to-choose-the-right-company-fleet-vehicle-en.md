## Begin with the job

The right fleet vehicle is the one that performs its assigned work safely and efficiently. Define passenger count, load, route, parking conditions, annual mileage and any access constraints before discussing make, model or trim. A vehicle chosen for status or headline price can become expensive if it does not fit the task.

## Segment users and routes

Separate urban field teams, long-distance sales users, executives, service crews and delivery operations. A compact hatchback may suit dense city work; a saloon can support sustained motorway travel; an SUV may offer access or seating advantages; a light commercial vehicle is appropriate when load space is a genuine requirement.

## Evaluate powertrain fit

Petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric vehicles behave differently across duty cycles. Compare energy cost, charging access, daily distance, payload, service coverage and expected resale or contract treatment. Avoid adopting one powertrain policy for every route without evidence.

## Check people, safety and ergonomics

Driver-assistance equipment, seating position, visibility and cabin comfort affect risk and fatigue. Test whether equipment can be carried and secured correctly. Include drivers and operational managers in a structured trial, but assess feedback against documented requirements.

## Decide with total cost

Compare rental or capital cost together with energy, maintenance, tyres, insurance, tax, administration and downtime. Standardising a smaller number of suitable models can simplify training and servicing, but excessive standardisation can create operational compromises. Review actual performance periodically and adjust the vehicle policy as roles change.

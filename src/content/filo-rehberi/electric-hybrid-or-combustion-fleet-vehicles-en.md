## There is no universal winner

Powertrain selection depends on route, distance, load, parking, charging, energy prices and service access. A fleet may achieve a better result by assigning different technologies to different duties.

## Battery-electric vehicles

Electric vehicles can offer efficient, quiet operation and lower routine mechanical maintenance. Their suitability depends on dependable charging, daily energy demand, seasonal range and time available to charge. Include infrastructure and public-charging effort in total cost.

## Hybrid vehicles

Full and mild hybrids differ materially. Some can reduce fuel use in stop-start operation without external charging, but benefit varies with route and driver behaviour. Evaluate the precise system rather than relying on the word “hybrid”.

## Internal-combustion vehicles

Petrol and diesel vehicles retain advantages where distances are unpredictable, rapid refuelling is critical or charging is not viable. Consumption, emissions, service needs and future operating restrictions should be tested over the planned term.

## Use a route matrix

Classify roles by daily distance, exceptional journeys, dwell time, payload and infrastructure. Compare realistic energy cost, rental or capital, maintenance, tax assumptions, downtime and contractual risk for each class.

## Review the transition

Pilot vehicles on representative routes and collect operational evidence. Technology, tariffs and infrastructure change; repeat the assessment at renewal rather than embedding an indefinite single-powertrain rule.

## Establish the fleet baseline

List every active and planned vehicle with its contract or ownership status, role, annual mileage, renewal date and current cost. Reconcile this register with finance records before forecasting.

## Separate cost categories

Build monthly lines for rentals or depreciation and finance, fuel or electricity, maintenance, tyres, insurance, taxes, tolls, parking, damage, replacement transport and administration. Keep VAT and other tax assumptions explicit and consistently applied.

## Forecast operational change

Include vehicle additions and returns, employee changes, new locations, seasonal demand and contract mileage revisions. Time each event by month; an annual total alone can conceal cash-flow pressure and delivery risk.

## Use volume and unit assumptions

Calculate energy from expected kilometres, consumption and unit price. Forecast maintenance and tyres from mileage and duty cycle. Document every material rate and source so the budget can be updated without rebuilding the model.

## Add scenarios and contingency

Prepare a base case and sensitivities for energy prices, mileage, damage and vehicle availability. Contingency should be linked to identifiable uncertainty, not used to hide unsupported estimates.

## Govern actual performance

Close each month by comparing actual cost and activity with budget. Separate timing differences from permanent variance and assign corrective actions. Reforecast the remaining year when operating assumptions change; the budget is a management tool, not a static annual document.
